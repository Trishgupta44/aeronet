## What this does and does not show

- **A PINN is trained per condition.** The inference time above is the cost of evaluating a network that was already trained for ONE shape, Mach number and angle of attack. It is a lookup of that one condition, not a solver. A new condition (another Mach number, angle or shape) means retraining, and the training times in the table are what that costs on this laptop.
- **The panel method needs no training.** Each new condition is a fresh solve of a few milliseconds. It is inviscid and linear (Prandtl-Glauert), so it does not capture the non-linear compressible effects the Euler PINN is built for.
- **The FVM times are quoted from another machine.** They come from Table 1 of the paper, are one solve each, were run on the authors' hardware and are not re-measured here; the paper itself says the table is "not meant as a rigorous comparison".
- **NeuralFoil solves a different problem** (viscous, incompressible, Re 6e6), so its time is a speed reference only and its Cl is not compared with ours.
- **No fair-speed claim is made beyond what is measured.** Accuracy at equal cost is a different question: it is answered by E2 (Cp error against the 800x400 FVM), not by this table. All times were measured on one laptop CPU with PyTorch; GPUs and other batch sizes were not measured.
